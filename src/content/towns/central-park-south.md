## What is actually standing on these three blocks?

An apartment-hotel frontage that stopped being hotels and never stopped being built like them.

The street took its present name in 1896 and runs three blocks from Grand Army Plaza at Fifth Avenue west to Columbus Circle. Between the wars it filled in almost all at once, and the type that filled it was the apart-hotel: a tower of small serviced rooms with kitchens, laundries and staff circulation designed into the base and the cellar. The Barbizon-Plaza at 106 Central Park South opened on 12 May 1930 with 1,400 ensuite rooms in thirty-eight stories. The Hotel St. Moritz at number 50, by Emery Roth in the same year, put a thousand rooms and six elevators into thirty-three floors at 365 feet. The Essex House at 160 broke ground on 30 October 1929 — the day after the Wall Street Crash — and opened forty-four stories on 1 October 1931. Hampshire House at 150 laid its cornerstone on 24 March 1931, stalled through the Depression and finally opened on 16 October 1937.

Now look at what those buildings are today. The Barbizon-Plaza's 1,400 rooms are 340 condominiums. Hampshire House holds 155 co-operative apartments. The St. Moritz was gutted between 1999 and 2002 and reopened with hotel floors below and eleven very large condominiums occupying the top twelve. The Essex House combines 426 rooms and 101 suites with 147 condominium residences, and a renovation in 1990–91 cut its room count from 690 to 591 while threading central air-conditioning through a frame that had been built without any.

That arithmetic is the whole page. A structure plumbed, ducted and vented for four or five small serviced rooms on every floor is now occupied by one or two households on that floor. The stacks did not leave. They were capped, boxed in, cut through, rerouted around new kitchens and then covered by the next renovation, and what survives behind the plaster is a set of vertical cavities that no drawing in the building's file describes accurately.

The neighborhood's housing figures show the same shape from the other side. Roughly 1,724 units, 1,245 of them — 72.2 per cent — in buildings of fifty or more. About 28.2 per cent of units date from 1939 or earlier, then a long trough through the middle of the century, then a second surge of 191 units in the 2000s and 188 in the 2010s as the supertalls went up behind the frontage. Tenure runs 79.2 per cent rented, and vacancy stands at 19.4 per cent — which on these blocks reflects apartments that are not anyone's primary home rather than apartments nobody wants.

## What does a park across the road actually change?

It replaces the neighbor with open ground, and open ground behaves differently.

In most of Manhattan the building next door is the reservoir. Here, on the north side, there is no building next door for two and a half miles. Central Park is 843 acres with eight lakes and ponds created by damming natural seeps, more than 303 recorded bird species, and an estimated 42 million visits in 2016. Directly inside Scholars' Gate at the Fifth Avenue corner sits the Pond, three and a half acres adapted from a historic mill stream, and beside it the four-acre Hallett Nature Sanctuary — the smallest woodland in the park, and about 250 feet from a residential lobby.

The Conservancy's own management decisions matter more here than anywhere else in the park, because they concentrate at the edge. It installed more than 700 purpose-designed garbage and recycling receptacles in 2013, patented, partly recycled aluminum, shaped specifically to hamper rodents climbing inside, and it states the principle without hedging: less trash means fewer rats in the park. But since Sheep Meadow in 1980 it has also pulled bins out of the interior landscapes and placed them on the paths, the Drives and the perimeter. That is sound landscape management and sound rodent management for the park's interior. It also means the waste of a very large visitor population is staged along the line where the park meets this frontage.

Add the carriage stand. On 12 August 2025 the Conservancy called for an end to horse-drawn carriages for the first time in its history, and among the three reasons its president gave was sanitation — operators failing to clear manure left daily on park roads, found on walks down West Drive between 72nd and 59th Streets. Feed and manure at a fixed stand is a reliable, replenished food source at curb level, and it sits opposite the buildings on this page.

NYC Health frames what rats need as food, water, shelter and safe ways to move around. On a normal Midtown block a building supplies some of those and denies others. Here the park supplies shelter, water and movement continuously and at scale, and the only variable a building actually controls is the fourth one. That is a narrower lever than most boards expect, and it is why work on this frontage weights the service side so heavily.

## Why does the back-of-house line on 58th Street run the job?

Because the front of a building on Central Park South is an elevation, and the back of it is an operation.

Look at what these structures contain besides apartments. The New York Athletic Club at 180 Central Park South, completed in 1929 and standing twenty-four stories, holds a swimming pool, a gymnasium, a basketball court, squash courts, a fencing and wrestling room, two boxing rings, two restaurants, a cocktail lounge and 187 overnight guest rooms. The Plaza, an eighteen-story steel frame with hollow tile floors built between 1905 and 1907, opened with more than eight hundred rooms served by ten passenger elevators and thirteen dumbwaiters, and its original plant ran nine 3,500-horsepower boilers on 750 tons of coal storage, made fifteen tons of ice a day, held 75,000 gallons of water and moved mail and kitchen orders by pneumatic tube. It now carries 282 hotel rooms and 181 condominiums in the same envelope.

None of that gets serviced from the park side. Deliveries, refuse, laundry, kitchen waste, staff circulation and plant access all resolve to the side streets and to West 58th Street behind. Within a few hundred feet you have banquet kitchens, club kitchens, hotel kitchens, restaurant kitchens and the loading and refuse arrangements for all of them, feeding buildings that also contain private homes on the floors above.

The consequence for a survey is specific. The productive places on this frontage are almost never in an apartment. They are the service corridor between a pantry and a riser closet; the goods lift lobby where a threshold has worn; the refuse room whose floor drain has lost its trap seal; the sub-cellar where a 1930s plant room was decommissioned and left; the escutcheon at the back of a stacked kitchen line where a supply pipe enters a wall. Cornell places German cockroaches in warm, high, dry gaps and crevices and notes that they arrive on food items and packaging — which is a description of a delivery route, not a housekeeping failure. American and Oriental cockroaches belong to the dark, damp end of the same building: basements, sewers, drains and waste pipes.

Boards on this frontage often ask for the residential floors to be treated more often. Frequency at the top of a building does nothing about a population living at the bottom of it. What changes the number is a refuse room that seals, a drain that holds water in its trap, a service door that meets its threshold, and closed penetrations where the stacks pass through each floor.

## Where does a converted apart-hotel actually leak?

At the joints between three generations of building services, and they are findable if you know what the building used to be.

**Redundant stacks and sleeves.** A floor that once served five rooms had five sets of supply and waste. A floor that now serves one apartment needs one. The others were cut, capped or simply abandoned in place, and a capped stack still leaves a hole through every slab it crossed. This is the single most productive [house mouse](/pest-library/house-mouse/) route in a converted building of this type, and it explains the pattern boards find inexplicable: activity confined to one vertical line of apartments while the flats either side report nothing.

**Retrofitted mechanical services.** The Essex House had central air-conditioning installed in 1990–91, sixty years after it was framed. Every building on this frontage has been rewired, recabled and fitted for data since. Each of those campaigns cut the shortest available path through a masonry structure, and nobody kept a record of where.

**Service stairs and goods circulation.** These buildings were designed so that staff, laundry, refuse and deliveries never met a resident. That produces a second circulation system with its own doors on every floor, its own lobbies and its own thresholds, used constantly and inspected rarely. Local Law 55 of 2018 requires door sweeps that reduce gaps to no more than a quarter of an inch on doors leading to a hallway, a basement or the outside. On a building with two full circulation systems, that clause covers roughly twice as many doors as anyone counts.

**Courtyards, terraces and setbacks.** 240 Central Park South, built in 1939–40 by Albert Mayer and Julian Whittlesey on a Bethlehem Steel skeleton, has a landscaped courtyard at its entrance, rooftop gardens and roughly a hundred cantilevered balconies across 387,428 square feet. 200 Central Park South, finished in 1963, is lined with tapering terraces on two faces of a curved elevation. Terraces are paved exterior floor over occupied space, and their drains, curbs and door thresholds are exterior conditions sitting above people rather than below them. [Pavement ants](/pest-library/pavement-ant/) work the joints; the drains carry the moisture that everything else follows.

**The small buildings between the towers.** Gainsborough Studios at 222 Central Park South is fifty feet of frontage and eight double-height stories, built by Charles W. Buckham in 1907–08 as an artists' co-operative with two duplexes and four single apartments per pair of floors. Wedged between much larger neighbors, it has party-wall conditions and a roof-to-roof relationship that a free-standing tower simply does not have. Buildings like it need a different survey from the one their neighbors need.

## Which services do these buildings actually need?

In the order this frontage generates them, which is not the residential order.

**[Rodent control](/pest-control/rodent-control/)** leads and splits cleanly. [Norway rats](/pest-library/norway-rat/) are a park-edge, curb and cellar problem: burrows in soft ground and planted beds, harborage under refuse staging, and entry from below through failed house traps and broken cellar drain lines. [House mice](/pest-library/house-mouse/) are the converted-hotel problem, established inside and distributed vertically through abandoned stacks. Cornell records a single mouse producing fifty to sixty droppings a day, which is why interior evidence appears abruptly in a building where the population has been in service space for years.

**[Structural exclusion](/pest-control/structural-exclusion/)** is what makes any of it hold. Here that means floor collars on live and dead stacks, service door lines, refuse room construction, the sub-cellar and drainage boundary, and the parapet and terrace thresholds at roof level.

**[Cockroach control](/pest-control/cockroach-control/)** divides by species and by level. [German cockroaches](/pest-library/german-cockroach/) are a kitchen-line and delivery-route program running through the service side. [American](/pest-library/american-cockroach/) and [Oriental cockroaches](/pest-library/oriental-cockroach/) belong to the drainage and the old plant rooms and respond to plumbing repair rather than to product applied upstairs.

**[Bed bug treatment](/pest-control/bed-bug-treatment/)** matters more here than in almost any residential market in the city, because of what shares the structure. Several hundred hotel rooms and 187 club guest rooms sit on these three blocks, and in the converted buildings transient and permanent occupancies stack vertically. [Bed bugs](/pest-library/bed-bug/) move through the same voids and conduit as everything else, so a report in a hotel floor is a question about the condominium floors above it from the first day.

**[Wildlife management](/pest-control/wildlife-management/)** covers the roof line. [Eastern gray squirrels](/pest-library/eastern-gray-squirrel/) have mature park canopy on one side of a hundred-foot roadway and an unbroken run of cornices, setbacks, terraces and roof gardens on the other. ## Which parts of Central Park South do we cover?

All three blocks and the service streets behind them, and each block behaves differently.

**Grand Army Plaza and the Plaza block.** The plaza was reconstructed to a Carrère and Hastings design in 1916, is bisected by 59th Street, and was designated a New York City scenic landmark on 23 July 1974. The Pulitzer Fountain, sixty-eight feet wide at its base, was installed around 1 May 1916; the Sherman Monument was dedicated on 30 May 1903. This is the most heavily used pedestrian ground on the frontage and it sits directly against the Plaza Hotel and Scholars' Gate.

**Fifth to Sixth Avenue.** The Plaza's block, and the eastern approach to Artists' Gate. Ground-floor and cellar commerce is dense and hotel service traffic is constant.

**Sixth to Seventh Avenue.** The old St. Moritz, now hotel and condominium in one structure, plus Hampshire House and the Essex House. This is the densest concentration of apart-hotel stock anywhere on the street and it holds Artisans' Gate at its western end.

**Seventh Avenue to Columbus Circle.** The New York Athletic Club, Gainsborough Studios, 200 and 240 Central Park South, Trump Parc, and Merchants' Gate at the circle. The widest range of building types and the only genuinely small building on the frontage.

**West 58th Street.** The service line for all of it — loading, refuse, staff entrances, plant access — and where most of the productive survey time is spent.

**West 57th Street and the towers behind.** 220 Central Park South took its first residents in late 2018: a 950-foot tower on 58th Street and the eighteen-story Villas facing the park, 118 apartments sharing a lobby and a three-story basement, on three concrete mats eight feet thick bearing on bedrock more than fifty feet down with 142 rock anchors. Deep, dry, recent basements are a different problem from 1930s sub-cellars, and they are not a smaller one.

## What does the law actually require of a building here?

More than most residents assume, and the duty follows ownership rather than occupancy.

The Housing Maintenance Code makes the presence of cockroaches, mice or rats in any room of a dwelling unit an immediately hazardous violation under section 27-2017.4, with twenty-one days from notice to correct. Section 27-2017.1 requires an owner to keep the premises free from pests and other indoor allergen hazards and to remediate expeditiously, including the underlying defect. Section 27-2019 puts the storage of building material, lumber and cartons that could afford harborage on both owner and tenants. The full reading sits at [NYC Housing Maintenance Code — extermination and rodent eradication](/compliance/nyc-housing-maintenance-code-pests/).

[Local Law 55 of 2018](/compliance/nyc-local-law-55/) converts that from a response into a schedule. Owners must investigate every occupied dwelling unit and the common areas at least once a year, and remediate using integrated pest management — defined in the statute as ongoing prevention, monitoring and pest control activities. The prescribed measures include inspecting for and physically removing pest nests, waste and debris by HEPA vacuuming or washing, eliminating points of entry, and fitting door sweeps to any door leading to a hallway, a basement or the outside so gaps are no more than a quarter of an inch. The statute then says plainly that pesticide use does not substitute for those measures, and that any pesticide must be applied by a professional licensed by the state — see [NYS pesticide applicator requirements](/compliance/nys-pesticide-applicator-requirements/).

The mixed-occupancy point is the one that catches boards out. Under Multiple Dwelling Law section 4, a hotel is a class B multiple dwelling — occupied, as a rule transiently, as the more or less temporary abode of individuals or families lodged with or without meals — while the apartments are class A. Where one structure holds both, as it does at several addresses on this frontage, a scope drawn around the residential condominium leaves the transient half of the same building feeding it. The two halves need one survey, whatever the two management structures look like on paper.

Every multiple dwelling, co-operatives and condominiums included, also files a bedbug annual report with HPD each December. Where material is applied, notification follows: [Local Law 36 pesticide notification](/compliance/nyc-local-law-36-pesticide-notification/) and [the New York neighbor notification law](/compliance/ny-neighbor-notification-law/). Boards should read [co-op and condo board obligations](/compliance/coop-condo-board-obligations/) and, before comparing bids, [choosing a pest control contractor](/compliance/choosing-a-pest-control-contractor/).

## What holds on a park-facing elevation, and what does not?

The materials question is where this work quietly succeeds or quietly fails, and on a designated or park-facing building it is also an appearance question.

Anything an animal can bite through is not a barrier, however convincing it looks on the day it goes in. Expanding foam is the most common error in the trade: rodents chew straight through cured foam, and a foamed opening reads as finished work for about one season. We do not put it in an assembly of ours in any role — not as the barrier, not as backing behind one, not as an air seal over one. Ordinary steel wool is the other frequent failure, because it rusts and streaks down a limestone or brick elevation that somebody is paying to keep clean.

What we install is custom-fabricated 26-gauge galvanised sheet metal, cut and bent to the opening rather than trimmed from a stock piece; copper and stainless mesh where an annulus has to be packed and a corroding fill would stain the face below it; hardware cloth wherever an opening has to keep passing air; mortar and hydraulic cement at masonry; Xcluder door sweeps and fill fabric at thresholds and around services; Xcluder GEO where a barrier has to sit below grade against burrowing; and mechanical fasteners throughout, because an adhesive bond is a guess about a surface nobody prepared.

Designation on this street is by individual building rather than by district. The Plaza was designated in December 1969, listed on the National Register in 1978, made a National Historic Landmark in June 1986 and had its interiors designated in July 2005. Gainsborough Studios was designated on 16 February 1988. 240 Central Park South was designated on 25 June 2002. Central Park itself has been a National Historic Landmark district since 1963 and a New York City scenic landmark since 1974, and Grand Army Plaza since July 1974. What that protection governs is the visible exterior. Cellars, sub-cellars, service stairs, riser closets, refuse rooms, courtyard service faces and the inner faces of parapets carry most of the work and rarely reach a review at all.

## When in the year should the work happen?

The frontage has a calendar, and it is not quite the city's.

**Autumn** is the highest-value window for the rodent side, and it is sharper here than inland because the pressure is coming off open ground rather than out of an adjoining cellar. Falling temperatures move animals from the park toward heated structure, and a cellar, drainage and threshold line closed in September does not acquire that population in November.

**Winter** is when the converted buildings give up their secrets. The heating system is running, the warm vertical columns are the routes the animals actually prefer, and the thermal leaks a cold day reveals are frequently the same openings a mouse is using. It is the right season to open a riser closet and follow a stack.

**Spring** is masonry season. Freeze-thaw damage on parapets, terrace curbs, courtyard paving and setback flashing becomes visible and is far cheaper to correct in April than to carry through another winter. Correcting it removes the water that drives concealed activity and stops a designated elevation deteriorating at the same time.

**Summer** is roof and terrace work: parapets, bulkheads, roof gardens, balcony drains and the wasp and hornet nests that reach full size at height in late summer. It is also the season to deal with standing water in planters and terrace drains within a few yards of where people sit.

The rule that overrides all four is that evidence is worth acting on the week it appears. In a building where one stack passes through forty floors, the interval between one apartment and six is measured in weeks.
